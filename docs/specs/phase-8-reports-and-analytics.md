Phase 8 — Reports and examination analytics
Goal. Examiners can understand examination performance through meaningful statistics, generate accurate PDF reports, and verify that a generated report is authentic. Reports must reflect actual persisted examination data rather than estimates or placeholder values.

This phase turns completed examination and grading records into useful, verifiable information without changing the underlying attempts, answers or results.
Build
- Examination performance dashboard. Provide examiners with an examination-level overview based on completed, eligible attempts. Show meaningful metrics such as total enrolled candidates, submitted attempts, participation rate, average score, highest score, lowest score and pass rate where a passing threshold is configured.

- Candidate performance breakdown. Present candidate-level results, including candidate identity, awarded marks, maximum marks, percentage and result status. Examiners can inspect individual candidate performance without exposing records belonging to examinations they do not own.

- Question-level analytics. Provide statistics for individual questions using actual submitted responses. Include response distribution, correct-response rate for objective questions, unanswered counts and other meaningful measures supported by the stored response data. Written questions should not receive automatically inferred correctness statistics.

- PDF report generation. Allow authorized examiners to generate downloadable PDF reports for an examination. Reports should contain the examination's relevant details, performance summaries and candidate-level results. The generated document must use persisted data and accurately represent the grading state at the time of generation.

- Report verification. Each generated report should have a unique verification reference. Provide a verification mechanism through which someone can establish whether a report was generated by Evalify and whether its verification reference is valid. Verification must not expose candidate records, answer sheets or other sensitive examination information.

- Report history. Preserve generated report records and their associated metadata, including the examination, generation time, generating examiner and verification reference. Authorized examiners should be able to identify and access previously generated reports.

- Report status and consistency. Clearly distinguish reports generated from finalized and published results from reports generated for internal examiner review, if draft reporting is supported. A report must never imply that unpublished results are available to candidates.
Constraints
- Persisted data is authoritative. Analytics and reports must be calculated from actual examination records, finalized attempts and the applicable grading data. Do not use hardcoded statistics, mock candidate records or frontend-calculated substitutes.

- Respect grading completeness. An examination with ungraded written responses must not present an incomplete score as a final candidate result. Distinguish objective marks already awarded from marks awaiting manual grading.

- Preserve result integrity. Generating or downloading a report must not modify examination attempts, answers, awarded marks or publication status.

- Enforce resource ownership. An examiner can only access analytics, candidate performance and reports for examinations they are authorized to manage. Administrators may access information only according to their established permissions.

- Protect candidate information. Reports may contain personal and academic information only where necessary for their intended audience. Public verification must never reveal candidate-level results or sensitive examination information.

- Use consistent calculations. Score percentages, averages, pass rates and participation rates must use clearly defined denominators and exclude attempts that are not eligible for official results, including sandbox attempts.

- Keep question analytics accurate. Objective-question statistics must use the immutable question and marking snapshots associated with completed attempts. Multiple-select correctness must follow the existing exact-set matching rule.

- Make report generation reliable. Repeated requests must not produce contradictory report records. A failed PDF-generation operation must not leave a report marked as successfully generated.

- Do not duplicate grading logic. Analytics and reports consume the established grading results. They must not introduce another implementation of objective grading or manual-mark allocation.
Acceptance checks
1. Complete an examination with multiple candidates and verify that the examiner sees accurate participation and performance statistics based on persisted attempts.

2. Compare the examination-level average, highest score, lowest score and pass rate against independently calculated values from the underlying candidate results.

3. Confirm that candidates with incomplete grading are not incorrectly included in finalized-score statistics.

4. Open the candidate performance breakdown and verify that each candidate's awarded marks, maximum marks and percentage match their finalized grading records.

5. Open question analytics for an objective question and confirm that response distributions and correctness rates reflect the actual submitted answers.

6. Verify that multiple-select analytics use exact-set matching and do not count partially correct selections as correct.

7. Generate an examination PDF report and confirm that its examination details, candidate results and performance summaries match the persisted application data.

8. Download a generated report and verify that it is readable, correctly formatted and contains no unrelated examination or candidate information.

9. Generate multiple reports and confirm that each report has a unique verification reference and a corresponding persisted report record.

10. Verify a valid report reference and confirm that the system identifies it as authentic without exposing confidential examination information.

11. Submit an invalid or modified verification reference and confirm that the system does not incorrectly authenticate the report.

12. Sign in as an examiner and attempt to access another examiner's analytics, candidate results and reports. All unauthorized requests must be rejected by the server.

13. Attempt to access a candidate's private results through the report or verification endpoints. Confirm that public verification cannot be used to retrieve private academic records.

14. Generate a report, subsequently change an examination's publication state or access another report, and confirm that the original report's generation record remains intact.

15. Generate a report while the PDF-generation process encounters an error. Confirm that the application communicates the failure and does not record an unsuccessful generation as a completed report.

16. Verify that sandbox attempts are excluded from official examination analytics, candidate performance summaries and generated official reports.

17. Refresh the analytics and report pages and confirm that their information is restored from persisted application data rather than depending on temporary frontend state.
Not in this phase
- Examination creation, editing, publication or candidate enrollment.
- Examination attempt creation, question delivery, answer saving, timing or submission.
- Examination monitoring, incident recording or examiner announcements.
- Automatic objective grading or manual grading of written responses.
- Changing, reviewing or publishing official examination results.
- Candidate-facing result publication or a new candidate results workflow.
- Examination sandbox implementation or sandbox attempt management.
- Bulk candidate and question imports.
- Changes to the existing question bank or examination marking configuration.
- AI-generated performance evaluations, AI grading or automated recommendations.
- External business intelligence integrations or third-party reporting services.
- Public access to detailed examination analytics or candidate-level information.
